## 1. Overview & Purpose

This assessment evaluates your deep understanding of the programming concepts and design decisions applied in your major project. Rather than a synchronous interview, you will create a pre-recorded screencast that demonstrates both your working project and your ability to analyze and justify your technical choices.

This format mirrors real-world professional expectations: explaining your code to teammates, justifying architectural decisions to stakeholders, and clearly communicating complex technical concepts.

## 2. What You Must Submit

**Important:** Your complete project code was already submitted as part of Assessment 2. This assessment (Assessment 3) requires **only a video submission**.

### **Single Submission: Two-Part Screencast Video**

You will submit one video file containing two distinct parts. **You must be visible via webcam ("picture-in-picture") throughout the entire recording.**

**Why the webcam requirement?**

- Verifies that YOU are presenting YOUR work
- Prevents use of AI-generated voices or having someone else present

**Total Duration:** 11-13 minutes recommended (maximum 15 minutes)

## 3. Video Structure Overview

Your screencast consists of two parts that will be recorded as a single, continuous video:

### **Part A: The Project Tour (5-7 minutes)**

This is a standard task for all students. You will provide a guided walkthrough of **the project you submitted in Assessment 2** that includes:

1. **Brief introduction** to what your project does and its purpose
2. **Code structure walkthrough** showing your file organization and key functions/classes
3. **Live demonstration** of your program running and showing its core features

**Important:** You will be presenting the code you submitted in Assessment 2. Open those files, show that code, and demonstrate that program running.

**For detailed guidance on Part A, including what to cover, time management tips, and examples, see the separate document:**  
**"Part A: Project Tour Guidelines"**

---

### **Part B: Analytical Response to Random Questions (4-6 minutes total)**

You will answer questions randomly assigned to you from a published question bank. Your questions will be drawn from three categories:

- **Category A: Core Concepts in Practice** - Testing foundational programming knowledge using your project
- **Category B: Design Choices & Justification** - Testing your ability to articulate the "why" behind your decisions
- **Category C: Critical Analysis & Reflection** - Testing your ability to evaluate your own work

**Assignment:** You will receive one question from Category A, one from Category B, and one from Category C. Each answer should be approximately 2 minutes.

**Timeline:**

- The complete **Question Bank** will be released on **[Date, e.g., Monday, Week 11 at 9:00 AM]**
- You will access your randomly assigned questions through the LMS activity: **"Generate My Viva Questions"**
- You may view your assigned questions as many times as needed

**For the complete question bank and detailed guidance on preparing strong answers, see the separate document:**  
**"Part B: Question Bank"** *(Available Week 11)*

---

### **How Part A and Part B Work Together**

Part A and Part B complement each other and assess different levels of understanding:

- **Part A (The Project Tour)** demonstrates your ability to present your work clearly and show that it functions correctly. You'll introduce your code structure, key components, and demonstrate features. Justifications here should be clear but concise—think "overview level."

- **Part B (Analytical Response)** demonstrates your ability to think critically and analyze your own work. You'll be asked to go deeper into topics that may have been introduced in Part A, discussing alternatives, trade-offs, limitations, and connecting your specific choices to broader programming principles.

**Example of progression:**

| Aspect | Part A Response | Part B Response (if asked) |
|--------|----------------|---------------------------|
| Code Organization | "I split my code into three modules: UI, data management, and calculations. This separation keeps concerns separate and makes testing easier." | "I chose modules over a single file or class-based architecture. A single file would be simpler but harder to maintain as the project grows. Classes would provide encapsulation but add complexity this project doesn't need. The trade-off is that modules require clear interfaces, which I implemented using... If this were a production system with multiple developers, I'd reconsider classes because..." |

**Key point:** Some Part B questions will deliberately ask you to expand on topics from Part A. This is not problematic repetition—it's an opportunity to demonstrate deeper analysis. Brief references to Part A are fine, but your Part B response should provide new insights and critical evaluation.

## 6. Marking Criteria & Rubric

Your submission will be assessed against the following criteria:

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Project Functionality & Demonstration** | 20% | Project works as described; core features are clearly demonstrated; code runs without critical errors |
| **Technical Justification & Design Reasoning** | 30% | Clear explanation of *why* specific design choices were made; demonstrates understanding of trade-offs; connects code to broader principles |
| **Analytical Response to Questions** | 40% | Answers demonstrate deep understanding; effectively links theory to personal project; provides specific, code-grounded examples; shows critical thinking |
| **Professional Communication** | 10% | Clear, well-organised presentation; appropriate pacing; effective use of visual demonstration; technically accurate language |

### **Detailed Rubric**

#### **Project Functionality & Demonstration (20%)**

- **17-20 (Excellent):** Project fully functional with all major features working; demonstration is comprehensive and polished; clear evidence of working program
- **13-16 (Good):** Project works with minor bugs; most features demonstrated effectively; generally functional
- **9-12 (Developing):** Project runs but has significant issues; incomplete demonstration; some features not working
- **0-8 (Poor):** Project doesn't run or critical features missing; minimal or no demonstration

#### **Technical Justification & Design Reasoning (30%)**

- **25-30 (Excellent):** Exceptional articulation of design rationale; discusses multiple alternatives and trade-offs; connects to unit concepts; shows depth of understanding
- **19-24 (Good):** Clear justification of major design choices; some discussion of alternatives; generally sound reasoning; adequate connection to concepts
- **13-18 (Developing):** Basic explanation of design choices; limited depth; some unclear reasoning; weak connection to broader principles
- **0-12 (Poor):** Cannot justify design decisions; explanations are vague or incorrect; no evidence of understanding design principles

#### **Analytical Response to Questions (40%)**

- **34-40 (Excellent):** Answers are insightful and code-grounded; demonstrates mastery; excellent use of specific examples from own project; shows critical thinking and self-awareness
- **26-33 (Good):** Solid answers that reference own code; accurate understanding; some depth of analysis; generally connects theory to practice
- **18-25 (Developing):** Answers are somewhat generic or superficial; limited connection to own work; some inaccuracies; minimal critical analysis
- **0-17 (Poor):** Answers are very generic, incorrect, or don't address the questions; no meaningful connection to own project; demonstrates lack of understanding

#### **Professional Communication (10%)**

- **9-10 (Excellent):** Exceptionally clear and professional; excellent pacing and structure; highly effective use of visual demonstration; polished delivery
- **7-8 (Good):** Clear and easy to follow; good organisation; appropriate visual support; competent delivery
- **5-6 (Developing):** Generally understandable but some unclear sections; adequate organisation; basic visual support
- **0-4 (Poor):** Difficult to follow; poorly organised; ineffective communication; inadequate visual support

## 7. Academic Integrity & Assessment Verification

### **Using AI and Other Resources**

You are **permitted and encouraged** to use AI tools (ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, etc.) to help develop your project and prepare your answers.

**However:** Your screencast must demonstrate *your understanding*, not regurgitated AI-generated content. We assess your ability to explain, justify, and critically analyse—not your ability to copy.

### **What Constitutes Academic Misconduct**

- Submitting a video where someone else is speaking or presenting your work
- Using AI-generated voices or text-to-speech instead of your own voice (this is why webcam is mandatory)
- Reading a script written by someone else or by AI without understanding it
- Having another person write your explanations
- Submitting a video about code that is not your own (from Assessment 2)
- Submitting code you cannot explain or defend

**The webcam requirement exists specifically to prevent AI voice generation and to verify you are presenting your own work.**

### **Assessment Verification Policy**

In accordance with [University Policy on Assessment Integrity / Policy Number if available], teaching staff reserve the right to require any student to attend a brief verification meeting to discuss their submitted work.

This is a standard academic practice to ensure assessment integrity and may be used at the discretion of the teaching team. If required, you will be contacted via your student email to arrange a suitable time.

**Important:** This is not an indication of suspected misconduct, but rather a quality assurance process that may be applied to any submission.

## 9. Preparing for Success

### **Study Strategy**

- Review the Part B Question Bank when released

- Focus on understanding *why* you made choices, not just *what* your code does

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I edit my video or do multiple takes?**  
A: Yes! This is pre-recorded, so you can do as many takes as needed and edit as desired.

**Q: Why is the webcam mandatory? Can't I just do a voiceover?**  
A: No. The webcam requirement serves two purposes: (1) it verifies that YOU are presenting YOUR work, and (2) it prevents the use of AI-generated voices. This is a core academic integrity requirement. Videos without visible webcam footage will receive a mark of zero.

**Q: What if my video is slightly longer than 13 minutes?**  
A: Minor variations (13-15 minutes) are acceptable. Videos significantly over 15 minutes will only be marked up to the 15-minute mark.

**Q: What if I get a question about something I didn't use in my project?**  
A: Category A (Conceptual) questions test your understanding of unit content generally. You should explain the concept and, where possible, relate it to programming principles. If a specific technique wasn't needed in your project, explain why you didn't need it.

**Q: Can I submit via a link (Loom/YouTube) instead of uploading the video file?**  
A: Yes, but you must submit a PDF document containing the link. Ensure the link is set to "unlisted" or "anyone with the link can view" and that it will remain accessible for at least 6 months.

**Q: What happens if I have technical problems on the deadline day?**  
A: Apply for an extension through official channels if you have a legitimate issue. However, you should plan to complete this well before the deadline. "My computer crashed at 4:55 PM" is not a valid excuse if you started the night before the due date.

**Q: Can I use notes during recording?**  
A: Yes, brief notes are fine, but your presentation should flow naturally. Reading directly from a script is obvious and will score poorly on communication. Remember: your face is visible, so we can see if you're just reading.

**Q: What if my project from Assessment 2 had bugs or didn't fully work?**  
A: Present it honestly. Explain what works and what doesn't. Your ability to analyse and discuss your code—including its limitations—is part of what we're assessing. Honesty about shortcomings often scores better than trying to hide problems.

**Q: Do both parts need to be in a single video file?**  
A: Yes. Record Part A, then continue recording directly into Part B. Do not submit two separate videos.

**Q: How do I access my random questions for Part B?**  
A: After the Question Bank is released in Week 11, navigate to the LMS section "Assessment 3" and click the activity named "Generate My Viva Questions." You will see your assigned questions and can return to view them anytime.
